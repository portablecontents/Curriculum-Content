BAD BUNNY

The Global Icon of Latin Trap & Reggaeton

📖

Biographical Synthesis

Born Benito Antonio Martínez Ocasio on March 10, 1994, in Vega Baja, Puerto Rico, he was raised in a working-class household alongside his two younger brothers. Benito began singing in his local church choir, eventually teaching himself music production while balancing shifts bagging groceries at an Econo supermarket and studying at the University of Puerto Rico at Arecibo.

Operating under the moniker Bad Bunny, he released his breakout trap single "Diles" on SoundCloud in 2016, sparking immediate industry demand. Refusing to alter his Puerto Rican accent or translate his catalog into English, Bad Bunny achieved absolute global hegemony, releasing genre-defining masterpieces like Un Verano Sin Ti and restructuring the modern pop marketplace entirely on his own cultural terms.

📀 Seminal Works

Bad Bunny's album rollouts stand as historic music industry records. His historic Un Verano Sin Ti (2022) became the **first all-Spanish album to earn a Grammy Album of the Year nomination** and spent 13 weeks atop the Billboard 200, alongside other blockbusters like YHLQMDLG (2020) and Nadie Sabe Lo Que Va a Pasar Mañana (2023).

The Streaming Emperor

❤️ Philanthropy & Social Impact

The Good Bunny Foundation (Founder)

Founded by Benito to improve the lives of Puerto Rican youth. The foundation donates millions directly to fund sports clinics, restore municipal athletic fields, distribute free art/music supplies, and host massive toy giveaways during Christmas.

Civic Protests & Social Justice

In 2019, Bad Bunny took a direct hiatus from his music career to physically lead massive street protests in San Juan alongside residents, successfully demanding the resignation of Puerto Rico's corrupt governor.

Grocery Bagger Beginnings

Just months before signing his major breakout deal, Benito supported his music hobby by bagging groceries at an Econo supermarket in Puerto Rico while recording on a basic laptop.

Spanglish SNL Auteur

In 2023, he made cultural television history by pulling double-duty as both host and musical guest on Saturday Night Live, delivering his entire opening monologue in Spanglish with Pedro Pascal.
